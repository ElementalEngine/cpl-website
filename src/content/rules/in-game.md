## Before the game

### Civ 6: standard game settings

These settings aren't voted on. They may only change by a majority vote, or by a host rule posted in text before
the vote.

- **Turn timer:** MPH Dynamic
- **Turn mode:** Simultaneous
- **Smart timer:** Competitive
- **Start era:** Ancient Era
- **World age:** New
- **City-states:** Firaxis default for the map size
- **Game speed:** Online
- **Map size:** Firaxis default for the number of players
- **Teamers start position:** Standard
- **Start position:** Balanced
- **Temperature, rainfall and sea level:** Standard
- **Game modes:** all off
- **Victory conditions:** all on
- **Turn limit:** by game speed
- **Duplicate civs and leaders:** allowed
- **Barbarians:** on in FFA, off in Teamers
- **Tribal villages:** on
- **Teams share visibility:** on
- **Map pins:** off (MPH)
- **No gold or strategic resource trading**
- **No military alliances**

### Diplomatic victory in Always War games

In any Always War game (FFA, Teamers or tournament), turn off the Diplomatic victory when the lobby is first
created.

### Custom host rules

- To add your own rule to a game, for example banning a pantheon, post the rule in #voting before you post the
  vote. This shows the rule wasn't made after people started voting. For example, it stops a host banning
  Fractal after seeing the votes for Fractal come in.
- Any host rule is allowed, unless it allows toxicity or cheating, or benefits one player or a minority.

### Novice games

CPL has a Novice role for newer, less experienced players of multiplayer Civ 6. Novice games give them a place to
improve and learn CPL's rules and ways of playing. Once staff think a player is experienced enough, the player
graduates to regular lobbies and can no longer use the novice section.

A novice game is a game that is either:

- staged, or partly staged, in a novice staging area, or
- advertised in any novice channel.

If a novice lobby doesn't have enough novice players to start, a non-novice may join as the last player in the
lobby, if the novice players agree unanimously in a secret vote. If the vote fails, another vote may only be held
once the novice players in the lobby change.

This rule is not meant for non-novices to play in novice games often. A game with a mix of novice and non-novice
players must be staged and advertised in the regular FFA channels.

### Backing out of a game

The game starts when the final plus is clicked on the game settings vote. If no settings vote is used, you may
leave without penalty until you click Ready in the game. After that, see
[Leaving a game](/rules/general/#leaving-a-game).

### Multiple drafts

If more than one draft is finished (all players clicked plus, or manual drafts), use the first draft the bot
printed. If nobody can tell which was first, the host calls a new draft.

### Draft trading

If a vote allows draft trading, players may trade their civ options with any other player, as long as both agree.
You must trade all of your draft picks.

### Drafting civs you don't own

- If your draft includes DLC civs you don't own, pick from the options you have left, or trade (see
  [Draft trading](#draft-trading)).
- If you don't own any civ in your draft, the host drafts you a civ separately with the `.draft-1` command, after
  everyone else has picked. You must pick the first civ in that draft that nobody chose and that isn't banned.

## During the game

### Public communication

Public communication must be in English. Using another language counts as private communication.

### Private communication

Game-related private communication between opposing players is not allowed.

### Turn timer changes

The turn timer may be changed during a game by a vote. It needs a majority of all players still in the game.

### Capturing city-states

- You may capture one city-state and keep it as your own city. If you lose it for any reason, you may not capture
  another one, unless it is a city-state you took before.
- You must raze any other city-state you attack.
- If another player has captured a city-state and owns it, you may capture and keep that city.
- In short, every player has 1 token to take a city-state. You can only use your own token once, but you can take
  other players' used tokens.
- A Great Person that hands a city-state to your empire doesn't count as capturing it.
- Never change the name of a captured city-state. A new name could hide that you captured one, to get away with
  capturing another.

### Civ 7: city-states

The city-state rules above don't apply to Civ 7. In Civ 7, players create city-states by becoming suzerain of an
independent power (a barbarian camp). There is no limit: players compete for them with influence.

### City trading

Trading cities between players is not allowed by default.

### Civ 7: settlement trading

In Civ 7, trading settlements is allowed (not with the AI), only as part of a peace deal.

### Liberating cities

You may liberate any city, as long as that doesn't bring a dead civilization back into the game. A civilization is
dead as soon as its last city is captured, and may not be brought back during the same turn. Dead city-states may
be
liberated and brought back into the game.

### Trading with AI civilizations

The AI often makes unbalanced deals, so trading with the AI is limited to:

- exchanging open borders
- renewing existing alliances
- peace deals
- ceding cities

Accepting delegations and embassies is also allowed. Every other deal is not: for example new alliances or
friendships that aren't renewals, or trading cities, strategic or luxury resources, gold, great works, relics or
diplomatic favour. Trade routes are not limited: players may send trade routes to anyone, including the AI, even in
No Diplo games.

### Friendships and alliances

- Friendships and alliances are limited to the number voted with the VoteBot. There are no exceptions, and
  friendships and alliances with the AI count towards that number. Because of this, you may start or renew
  friendships and alliances with the AI.
- Extra unofficial friendships and alliances are allowed, but they are not binding.

### Relic trading

In Teamers games, relics may not be traded before turn 20. From the start of turn 20 you may trade them with your
teammates.

### Great People

- Recruit or pass a Great Person before the last part of the turn.
- Passing a Great Person so late in the turn that an opponent auto-recruits a Great Person they didn't want is
  unsportsmanlike conduct.
- Auto-recruiting a Great Person to abuse the slot order is unsportsmanlike conduct.
- Once half the turn has passed, any player with the option may be asked to recruit or pass. After war moves, this
  must be the first action of their turn. With a quarter of any player's turn left, the game may be paused, and it
  only resumes when the player with the option has announced their decision.
- **Teamers:** a team that was forced to auto-recruit an unwanted Great Person, or couldn't recruit one because of
  the slot order, is entitled to a relobby.
- **FFA:** Great Person problems are not a reason for a relobby. Not recruiting or passing a Great Person for a
  long time is unsportsmanlike conduct.

### AFK players

- If you think a player has gone AFK, pause the game in the last 20 seconds of the turn. Ask the player to answer,
  both in the game chat and out loud in the Discord voice channel. If they don't answer in the game chat within
  5 minutes, the host must kick them at the start of the next turn. Report a player kicked for being AFK as a
  quitter at the end of the game.
- Going AFK or quitting for less than 10 minutes more than once, or calling this rule too often in the same game,
  may be unsportsmanlike conduct. Moderators decide.
- You don't need this rule during breaks or pauses for votes, which are AFK time anyway. After a break, players
  count as AFK 1 minute after it ends.
- The AFK rule doesn't apply to players who disconnected. For disconnects, see the
  [drop policy](/rules/general/#drop-policy).
- A player who was AFK but not kicked may still be punished, based on the game log or the turn saves.

### Infinite turns

In an infinite turn, players have 5 minutes to finish their turn. After that, the host may force the turn to end
with MPH. Any other use of force end needs a moderator's approval.

### Intentional feeding

You may not speed up your own elimination, for example by not defending yourself or inviting others to conquer you.
This is worse than quitting, because you put up less of a fight than an AI would. How you vote in the World
Congress is never counted as feeding.

### Stream sniping

Watching a stream of a game you are playing in is not allowed.

### Members in voice who aren't playing

A CPL member in the voice channel of a game they aren't playing must mute or deafen if a player in the game asks.

## Relobbies

### Repeat your moves after a relobby

If the game is relobbied to the start of the turn or to an earlier turn, players must repeat their actions.
Different important actions, such as war moves, recruiting Great People, or chopping walls or wonders, may lead to
a
relobby, an infraction or both.

### Desyncs

- A player who is desyncing and at war may ask for a pause to resync. Any player who sees or hears the request must
  pause the game. Not pausing leads to a relobby at the start of the turn, with completely new war moves for every
  player involved.
- Once the game carries on (with or without a pause), a relobby can't happen.
- If a desyncing player is at war, any player in that war may ask for a relobby, and it is granted after the next
  turn rollover.

### Asking for a relobby

To ask for a relobby for something that doesn't normally need one, see
[Relobby votes](/rules/general/#relobby-votes).

## End of the game

### Victory screen ties

If 2 players get a legitimate victory on the same turn, the player higher in the slot order gets the victory
screen, but the result counts as a tie for 1st.

### Leaving at the end

The host says when a vote has ended the game. See [Leaving a game](/rules/general/#leaving-a-game).
